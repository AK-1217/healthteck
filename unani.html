The Greek-Arabic System of Medicine

Unani medicine, also known as Unani-Tibb, is a system of medicine that originated in Greece and was greatly developed during the Arab civilization. It is based on the teachings of Hippocrates and Galen, later enriched by Islamic scholars.

Unani medicine is built on the concept of the four humors — blood (Dam), phlegm (Balgham), yellow bile (Safra), and black bile (Sauda). Health is defined as a state of equilibrium among these humors.

Key Principles

  • Arkan: Four elements — Earth, Air, Water, Fire.
  • Mizaj: Temperament of the individual.
  • Akhlat: The four humors.
  • Ilaj bil Tadbeer: Regimental therapy (diet, massage, cupping).

Classical Texts

Important Unani texts include the Kitab al-Mansuri by Rhazes, Al-Qanun fi al-Tibb (The Canon of Medicine) by Avicenna, and treatises by Hakim Ajmal Khan.

Quick Fact

Unani medicine is widely practiced in India, with dedicated colleges and research councils.
